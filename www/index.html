Exam Tips & Study Plan

🎯 During the exam

  • Answer the easy items first. Don't get stuck. If an item takes more than about a minute, mark it, move on, and come back later.
  • Use elimination. Cross out the choices that are clearly wrong. Removing two choices doubles your chance of getting the item right.
  • Plug in the choices. For algebra items, try substituting each answer choice into the equation. It's often faster than solving.
  • Estimate first. In math, round the numbers to see which choice is reasonable before computing exactly.
  • Reading passages: read the question first, then the passage, so you know what to look for. The answer is always supported by the text, not by outside opinion.
  • Watch the key words: NOT, EXCEPT, LEAST, MOST, ALWAYS. Many students miss items just by misreading these.
  • Blank vs. guess: read the instructions. If wrong answers are not penalized, never leave an item blank. If there is a penalty, guess only when you can eliminate at least one choice.
  • Shade carefully. Check that the item number on your answer sheet matches the question, especially after skipping items.
  • Watch the clock. Check the time at the quarter, half, and three-quarter points of each subtest.
💡 Mock Exam has a ⚑ Mark for review button so you can practice the skip-and-return habit.

🎒 Before exam day

  • Prepare everything the test permit requires, usually the permit, a valid ID, No. 2 pencils, an eraser and a sharpener. Follow your university's exact list.
  • Visit or locate the testing center a day before. Know the travel time from Bataraza so you arrive at least 30–45 minutes early.
  • Sleep 7–8 hours the night before. A rested mind scores better than a crammed one.
  • Eat a light, filling breakfast and bring water and a small snack if allowed.
  • Do a light review of formulas the night before. Don't learn new topics anymore.

🎯 Target: score 75% or higher on the MSU-SASE style mock at least twice before the actual exam.

🧠 Study habits that work

  • Test yourself. Answering questions builds memory better than rereading.
  • Spread it out. One hour a day for four weeks beats ten hours the night before.
  • Learn from mistakes. Read every explanation, especially for items you guessed right.
  • Study with a buddy. Explain an answer to a classmate. If you can teach it, you know it.
  • Read in English daily. News articles and stories build vocabulary and reading speed for every subtest.
